Practical advice

  • Eat all your food as meals, not individual components (i.e., don’t just eat straight grains or straight meat, you make them into a meal)
  • A meal is 1/2 plate of calorie containing food, 1/2 plate of vegetables
  • Weigh your vegetables on a scale. You want 100g vegetables to 100g of calorie-containing food. 100g of vegetables is way more than you think. 100g of calorie-containing food is way less than you think.
  • Macronutrients and any of that stuff is just marketing magic that you don’t understand, so they can make you think it matters. It doesn’t.
  • Your entire diet should basically be:
    1. whole grains
    2. legumes
    3. non-starchy vegetables
    4. fruits
  • You build meals out of those and you don’t eat other things. You use seasoning blends and artificial sweeteners to make it taste good. You do not use oil or sugar.
  • If you want to lose weight, you increase the amount of vegetables in your meal by replacing the denser foods and the other things will naturally decrease. That is just how the body works.
  • Consistency teaches your body about the calorie content of its environment. This is what matters

Summary

Excess calorie intake literally drives all of our health problems in America

Calorie density of foods is all that matters, the lower the density of the foods in your diet, the lower your body weight will be and the less problems you will have

If you look at a nutrition label, you want your food to be <1.5 calories/gram, so like if you have a 100 gram serving of food, you want it to be less than 150 calories at the most

If you just think about the human body, we literally have a maximum physical capacity, so the idea is you fill that capacity with fewer calories and that’s how you actually control intake

The energy intake rate is what matters practically, but that’s much harder to quantify, so just think "if this is slow to eat and has a low calorie density, then it is a good choice", so like liquid calories have an extremely rapid energy intake rate, even with a low calorie density, that makes them a terrible choice, but carrots have a very low energy density and a very slow eating rate, so you use them in meals to slow intake of denser foods, like whole grains

When you eat huge volumes of food, your body weight on the scale will increase in the short term. When you fill your body with more matter, that results in a higher scale weight. It is also how you actually lose weight/body fat. So, you simply have to trust the science and suck it up in the short term, it will pay off. There is not a chance that you will eat more calories of donuts than a grain/lentil/vegetable/fruit artificially sweetened meal. Your body just physically will not allow it. It does not matter what your brain tells you. You ate fewer calories than you would have otherwise

Study: High vs Low Calorie Density (1)

https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41591-020-01209-1/MediaObjects/41591_2020_1209_MOESM1_ESM.pdf

Summary:

Diets eaten to satiety by people on a high calorie density vs low calorie density diet

Takeaways:

  • Low calorie density diet resulted in 700 less calories eaten per day
  • Calorie density is all that matters

Study: Diet, Lifestyle, and Weight Gain

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3151731/

Summary:

Tracked diet and lifestyle of 130,000 people over 20 years and saw what was healthy, what caused weight gain, what was protective

Takeaways:

  • More exercise, less weight gain, more TV, more weight gain
  • Don’t eat potatoes, corn, peas
  • Don’t eat meat

Study: Fruits and Vegetables

https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1001878

Summary:

Tracked fruits and vegetable intake of 130,000 people over 20 years and saw what fruits and vegetables caused weight gain, what was protective

Takeaways:

  • Calorie density of fruits/vegetables is all that matters
  • Just eat apples and berries really, other fruits are fine, but apples and berries far and away the best
  • All non starchy vegetables are basically the same, so just whatever is cheapest

Study: Artificial Sweeteners

https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-025-01381-z

Summary:

Followed a bunch of people over a year, those who used artificial sweeteners were significantly healthier than those who didn’t

Takeaways:

  • If you use artificial sweeteners to improve your diet quality, that drastically outweighs any potential negative of it’s use
  • Use artificial sweeteners to eat vegetables and foods you don’t like — it is better 100% of the time than eating something with sugar or added fat in it
